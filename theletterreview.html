The Letter

Score: 8.9

Platform: PC, Mobile, Console, Nintendo Switch
Genre: Horror, Visual Novel, Indie
Release Date: July 25, 2017
Reviewed by: Mirabelle Tena

Pros

  • Seven Playable Perspectives
  • Full Voice Acting
  • Filipino Craftsmanship

Cons

  • Slow Opening Chapters

Full Review

I went in expecting a fairly standard haunted-house story and came out having lived through seven of them at once. The Letter drops you into the Ermengarde Mansion through the eyes of characters who don't trust each other, don't share the same information, and sometimes don't even survive to the next chapter. Watching their versions of the same night contradict and complete each other is where the game earns its reputation.

The horror itself leans more atmospheric dread than jump-scare theater, closer in spirit to Japanese horror cinema than a Western slasher. Sound design carries a lot of that weight creaking floors and distant whispers do more work than any single visual. The character writing is where it truly shines though; these aren't horror - movie archetypes waiting to be picked off, they argue, lie, and make understandable bad decisions the way real people under pressure do. My one real complaint is pacing. The early chapters ask for patience before the mystery starts folding in on itself, and with branching choices scattered across seven routes, it's easy to feel like you're managing a spreadsheet instead of a story. Once the threads start connecting though, that complexity turns into the game's biggest strength. For a first title out of a small Filipino studio, The Letter is an ambitious, confident piece of horror storytelling that's well worth the time investment.
